# Russia's state finance, arms sales and companies

Money that reaches Russia outside ordinary trade: payments for Russian nuclear projects and Russia's state loans, purchases of Russian weapons, and money moved by companies. Each is a yearly change against the pre-war level and shown in the country breakdown like any other part.

Details for developers: [sources, cases and research files](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/war-balance/blob/main/docs/reference/method/russia-state-and-companies.md).

## Nuclear projects and state loans from Russia

- **Nuclear fuel** is already in goods trade: nothing added.
- **Construction by Rosatom** (Egypt, Bangladesh, Hungary's Paks II, India, Iran, Uzbekistan) is in no trade data. Only the buyer's own share counts; the part paid with a Russian state loan is Russian money paying a Russian company.
- **Repayments of Russia's state loans** count only where they deviate from the pre-war schedule: a loan ending on schedule is not a war-time choice.
- **Customs over-counts removed**: equipment for Türkiye's Akkuyu plant is imported by Rosatom's own Turkish company.

| country | on the map, EUR bn (toward Ukraine +) | why |
|---|---|---|
| Türkiye | +0.61 | customs over-count of Akkuyu equipment removed |
| Hungary | −0.78 | Paks II payments and loan prepayments (certainty D) |
| Viet Nam | +0.71 | stopped paying in 2023 (blocked payment channels) |
| Iraq | +0.37 | stopped paying |

Arrears caused by sanctions on payment channels still count (the money did not reach Russia); the panel says so. Belarus's deferrals were refinanced with new Russian loans, so they are left out.

## Weapons bought from Russia

Russia's arms sales fell from about USD 15bn a year in 2019–2021 to 7–9bn after 2022. Arms are almost entirely outside customs data, so they are a part of their own, split by buyer from contracts and SIPRI data, against a no-war level without one-off contracts. They count at Russia's net gain on arms (about 0.04–0.05 of the price): India +0.19bn, China +0.18, Egypt +0.13, Algeria +0.10, Iran −0.07. Much of the fall was not the buyer's choice (Russia kept its output for its own army); the panel says so. Mostly educated guesses (certainty D; India and China C).

### More buyers

18 more buyers come from SIPRI fact sheets (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Libya, Sudan, Ethiopia, Laos, Malaysia, Venezuela and others). Iran is counted once: part of its payments was settled in drones and missiles already counted as weapons sent to Russia.

## Companies leaving Russia

**Not in the totals**; a marker on the map and a column in the full data download ([below](#view=how&sec=m-russia-state-and-companies-taken-by-russia-not-in-the-totals)). By the company's home country:

- the **exit fee** Russia's budget requires for every sale since 2023;
- the **forced discount**: sales at least 50–60% below value hand the difference to Russian buyers (Renault, Société Générale, McDonald's, Shell...).

## Assets seized by Russia

Also out of the totals, as a marker: company assets taken under temporary-management decrees (Uniper's Unipro, Fortum, Danone, Carlsberg), expropriation (Wintershall Dea, OMV, ExxonMobil, Shell), court seizures (Raiffeisen, UniCredit, Deutsche Bank, Linde) and leased aircraft kept by Russian airlines. About EUR 33bn (22–47), at the owners' own write-downs. Largest: Germany 9.5bn, the US 4.9, the UK 3.9, Austria 3.4, Ireland 2.8 (aircraft lessors).

## Taken by Russia, not in the totals

Seizures and forced exit costs are a loss imposed on the country's companies and a gain for Russia, but **the country did not choose to move that money; Russia took it**. So they are in neither headline number. They appear as a square marker (layer "Taken by Russia") where Russia took at least 0.1% of a year's GDP, as a line in the panel, and in columns marked "not in totals" in the downloads. Largest, 2022 to August 2026 (EUR bn, seizures + forced exits): Germany 9.5 + 2.4, the US 5.0 + 5.2, France 0.7 + 6.1, the UK 3.9 + 3.9.

## Russian-owned companies abroad

Profits Russian parents receive from their companies abroad, against 2019–2021: Lukoil's refineries (Bulgaria, Romania, the Netherlands), NIS in Serbia, Rosneft in India, Russian banks. Trusteeships that stopped transfers count toward Ukraine: Germany (Rosneft Deutschland, Gazprom Germania) about EUR 1.3bn. Metals, mining and chemicals plants (Rusal's Aughinish in Ireland, KUBAL in Sweden, NLMK, EuroChem, Uranium One) send little home: the change stays within ±EUR 0.05bn a year. Mostly estimates (D), except Serbia.

## Frozen central-bank reserves

About EUR 260bn of the Bank of Russia's reserves have been frozen since 28 February 2022, about 185bn at Euroclear in Belgium. The frozen amount itself is not counted. What counts is **the yearly return those reserves would have earned for Russia**, about EUR 31bn over 2022 to August 2026: money Russia did not get.

- **A line of its own**, "Russian money frozen", added to the net only. It is not a payment, so it is not in Russia's earnings from trade; and it is not something given to Ukraine, so not in support for Ukraine.
- **Who gets it**: the freeze is an EU decision, so two thirds of Euroclear's and Clearstream's return go to all EU members by their share of EU aid, and one third to the host (Belgium, Luxembourg), which carries the legal risk. The one third is a judgement.
- Belgium's tax on Euroclear's Russian-asset income is already in its aid, so it is taken off first. The G7 loans repaid from this return are left out of donors' aid, so nothing is counted twice.

Largest, 2022–2026: Belgium EUR 7.4bn (1.2% of GDP), France 5.0, Germany 3.2, Italy 1.9, the UK 1.8.
