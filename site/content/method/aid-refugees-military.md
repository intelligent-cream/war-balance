# Aid, refugees, military support and flowback

The Ukraine side of the ledger: government aid (Kiel Institute and, for the 190 countries outside its tracker, UN and government records), each EU member's share of EU-level aid, undisclosed military aid, private donations and the cost of hosting refugees. On the Russia side, weapons and troops sent to Russia. And in between, flowback: aid that returns to the donor's own economy as orders for its own industry. This page explains each, including the corrections that keep Russia's own money (loans repaid from frozen assets) out of donors' aid.

## Aid

**Round 24b (audit 24, D M4; approved by the owner 22 October 2026): military aid counts when delivered.** Kiel records military aid when it is allocated; much of it, above all what is bought from industry, arrives years later. Each Kiel military allocation row is now counted as delivered evenly over a lead time from its start date: donor stocks 0.25 years, NATO's PURL 0.5, procurement in Ukraine (the "Danish model") 1, other procurement 3, and US USAI 4.5, set so that the model gives 57.6% of USAI delivered by March 2026 against the Department of Defense's 57% (Lead IG quarterly report, read via Militarnyi and Legis1). What was allocated but not yet delivered by 31 August 2026 comes off the aid (Germany 4.3bn, the US 10.2bn, Denmark 2.0bn; the EU's own military aid, mostly the EU Support Loan, by each member's EU share); Kiel's full allocation is the high end of support and the slow lead times the low end. Germany's own cash payments to March 2025 (EUR 15.07bn, federal list of 31 March 2025) exceed Kiel's German procurement allocations then (EUR 8.3bn), so for Germany the smaller (fast-delivery) cut is central. The money a donor spends at home (flowback "own") follows the same delivered share. Data: [`military_delivered.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/military_delivered.json), written by `pipeline/military_delivered.py` from the Kiel workbook; [notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/military_delivered-notes.md). The panel's aid card says how much was not yet delivered.

Data: [`kiel.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/kiel.json) ([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/kiel-notes.md)), the [Kiel Institute Ukraine Support Tracker](https://www.kielinstitut.de/research/topics/war-against-ukraine/ukraine-support-tracker/), Release 31 (data 24 January 2022 to 31 August 2026), allocations by month, categories military, financial and humanitarian. Code: aid section of [build_data.py `build()`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/pipeline/build_data.py). <span class="incode">In the code: `Allocations per Month`</span>

- **Allocated, not pledged.** "Allocated" means delivered or contracted. Commitments not yet allocated are shown as `pledged_open` in "More details", not counted.
- **Loans** count at their **economic value to Ukraine** in the net shift and at the **lender's cost** in the cost view (not shown on the site since round 19; in the full data download), both per loan item from measured rates and expected losses (round 12, [loans](#view=how&sec=m-loans); certainty C).
- **ERA loans excluded.** G7 loans repaid from immobilised Russian assets (ERA: US 18.3, EU 18.1, Canada 3.3, UK 2.6, Japan 1.6 EUR bn) are Russia's money, not the donor's. They are taken out of the donor's years (in the year paid: US 2024, Japan 2026, UK 2025–2026, the others 2025), loans and financial category; [`check_data.py`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/pipeline/check_data.py) check 5 verifies this. <span class="incode">In the code: `era_excluded` in `data.json`</span>
- **Amounts without a month** (Italy 1.3bn) are spread over the years in proportion.

**Outside Kiel** (190 countries): [`aid_gaps.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/aid_gaps.json) ([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/aid_gaps-notes.md)) from the [UN OCHA Financial Tracking Service](https://fts.unocha.org/countries/234/summary/2026) and official statements. Largest: Saudi Arabia 0.37bn, UAE, Qatar, North Macedonia (military), Azerbaijan. 163 countries with no record anywhere get 0 with a small upper bound (0.002% of 2022 GDP, a judgement: [decisions](#view=how&sec=cl-r11)), so no one is assumed to have given nothing for certain. Check 2 verifies no country takes aid from both Kiel and the gap research.

### EU-level aid

Aid from EU institutions (Commission, Council, EIB; Kiel's `EU_INST`) is split over the 27 members by their share of EU budget contributions, Kiel's own method, and shown as its own part, "Share of EU-level aid", with the same relative range as the member's aid. Check 1 verifies the members' shares add up to the EU total. <span class="incode">In the code: `eu_share`</span>

**The 2026 EU Ukraine Support Loan** is guaranteed by 24 members, not Czechia, Hungary or Slovakia; their slice of the 2026 disbursements goes to the other 24 in proportion (`apply_audit()`, [audit-notes.md](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/audit-notes.md) item 9; Czechia 2.06 → 1.90, Hungary 0.98 → 0.87, Slovakia 1.37 → 1.30 EUR bn).

**The European Peace Facility (EPF) is not counted** (round 16, review 5 L1, approved by the owner). Kiel has not counted it since tracker version 16 ([kiel-notes.md](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/kiel-notes.md)): the EPF reimburses member states for weapons they gave Ukraine themselves, which are already in their own bilateral aid, so adding it would count them twice. It is not paid from the EU budget either: it is off-budget, funded by the member states by a GNI key, so it shifts who bears the cost of those weapons between members; the map does not model that shift ([limitations](#view=how&sec=limitations)). The panel says so, and gives the member's EU-level share at the same value as its bar (loans at their gift to Ukraine), with the face value beside it (until round 15 the text showed the face value, Lithuania €294m against €211m on the bar).

**Grade of the adjusted aid** (round 16, review 5 L4): Kiel's figure is reported (A), but the part on the map is adjusted by modelled factors (weapons at market value, loans as gifts). Its letter is A only where those adjustments are under 5% of it, B under 25%, else C. <span class="incode">In the code: `aidCert` in `calc()`</span>

### Donated weapons at market value (round 5)

Donors value weapons from their stocks differently: the US at replacement cost until March 2023 and at depreciated book
value after (GAO-24-106934: about USD 8.1bn overstated), the UK budget at replacement cost (National Audit Office: book
value GBP 0.17bn against replacement GBP 2.7bn), Nordic and Baltic EU Peace Facility claims at 91–100% of the new price.
The map counts the **value to Ukraine: what the equipment would fetch on the market**. Each Kiel row gets a factor
(market value ÷ recorded value) by how it was valued and what it is (MiG-29 × 0.12, F-16 × 0.30, Patriot × 0.75, shells
× 1.3 because their price doubled); new procurement keeps factor 1. Code: `apply_weapons_value()` stores yearly
corrections, which `calc()` adds to aid in the shift toward Ukraine. **The cost view (in the full data download <span class="incode">In the code: `aid.weapons_mv`</span>
only since round 19) keeps the recorded values**, closer to what the donor spent. Data: [`weapons_value.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/weapons_value.json)
([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/weapons_value-notes.md)).

Over 2022–2026 this lowers bilateral military aid by about 14% (EUR 177bn → 152bn): USA −7.0bn, UK −2.7, Sweden −2.4,
Poland −1.8, France −1.6, Netherlands −1.3, Norway −1.1; Germany only −0.5 (mostly new procurement). The correction
shrinks over time as aid moved from stocks to new production.

### Loans at their economic value (round 5)

A loan that is repaid is worth less to Ukraine than a grant. Since round 12 each loan and guarantee counts at its gift:
cheaper interest, discounted at Ukraine's normal 2021 rate (6.7%), plus the default risk the lender really carries (the
measured expected loss of its creditor class: 0 for the IMF and World Bank, for EU loans the provisioning rate the EU set for each loan (70% for the 2022 exceptional MFA and the repurposed EIB loans, 9% for the rest), the bilateral standstill
for G7 and Paris Club lenders, about 95% for the 2026 Support Loan). The high end discounts at the promised war yield
of the year of payout (38% in 2022, 40% in 2023, 15–24% after). Round 5 used a flat 10% (6–15%), a judgement. Over
2022–2026 face value EUR 96.5bn becomes about 55bn (42–85); it was 64bn (40–77). The full method, the measured rates,
a worked example and the evidence are on the [loans page](#view=how&sec=m-loans). Code: [`loan_rates.py`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/pipeline/loan_rates.py)
and `apply_loans_value()`; data: [`loans_value.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/loans_value.json)
([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/loans_value-notes.md)). The cost view (in the full data download only) uses the lender's cost: the same terms and
losses at the lender's own funding rate ([loans](#view=how&sec=m-loans-the-cost-view)).

**Multilateral lending** (IMF, World Bank, EBRD, CEB): only the rise over each institution's 2019–2021 own-risk lending
counts (donor-guaranteed and trust-fund lending is already in Kiel), valued the same way and credited by shareholding,
as its own part ([loans](#view=how&sec=m-loans-new-lending-by-the-imf-and-world-bank)). Biggest: US 0.28bn, Germany 0.13, Japan
0.12, France 0.11, Italy 0.10, China 0.09 (round 5: US 0.66, Japan 0.27).

**ERA years** (round 5): the ERA loans (repaid from frozen Russian assets, left out) are removed in the years they were
paid: the US in 2024, Japan in 2026, the UK 1.78bn in 2025 and the rest in 2026, the others in 2025; the UK's is coded
as military in Kiel ([refresh_2026-notes.md](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refresh_2026-notes.md)).

## Refugees

**Round 24b (audit 24, D H1; approved by the owner 22 October 2026): hosting at actual spending, minus only the host's gain on refugees' work.** Until round 24b the hosting cost was net of all the taxes and contributions working refugees pay, floored at zero; that set Czechia, Slovakia, Romania and the Baltic states to nothing and broke the one-seller rule: refugees' wages, and the taxes on them, are their own income, not the host's gain (the same reason remittances are not counted). Now hosting = what all levels of government actually spent on refugees from Ukraine, minus donor-paid shares, minus the host's **real gain on refugees' work**: refugees' wage bill × the host's net gain per euro of their wages in the industries they work in (taxes on production plus profit above a normal return, `net_gain_sellers.json`, × output per euro of labour cost, OECD STAN) × the genuinely extra share (0.5, range 0.3-0.8). No floor. The industries are measured for the Netherlands (CBS) and ranked for Germany, Poland and Czechia (Bundesagentur für Arbeit, ZUS, MPSV); Ireland takes the OECD median margins (multinational profit booking). **Poland:** its own STAN margins give a gain per euro of wages five times Germany's, so the owner chose the OECD median at central and Poland's own margins (its central case) as the range end. Effect: Czechia's hosting 0 → EUR 3.1bn, Germany's 19.7 → 35.5bn, Poland's 4.9 → 10.6bn. Data: [`refugees_gain.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees_gain.json), [notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees_gain-notes.md). The items below describe the earlier rules; item 4's tax netting and floor no longer apply. The money spent hosting (rent, food, retail) still flows back to the host (below), now on the gross spending, not on the counted cost.

Hosting refugees from Ukraine counts toward Ukraine. Sources, in order of preference:

1. **Government budgets and audit offices** for 27 hosts ([`round4b.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/round4b.json) §2, [notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/round4b-notes.md)), applied by `apply_audit()`: gross public spending on Ukrainians per calendar year (US ORR and benefits, German federal budget and Bürgergeld, Polish Aid Fund, UK Homes for Ukraine, Dutch central government, Swiss lump sums and others). 2022–2026 total EUR 133bn against 155bn in the earlier model: the US (−7.4), Canada (−5.2), Spain, Italy and the UK fall; Germany (+2.0) and Norway (+1.0) rise.
2. **Elsewhere**, [`refugees.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees.json) ([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees-notes.md)): person-years per calendar year (Eurostat `migr_asytpsm` monthly stocks for the EU and EFTA, UNHCR end-year counts with a ramp elsewhere, US and Canadian programme arrivals) × k × GDP per head, with k calibrated on the reported budgets (certainty C).
3. **Donor-paid costs** are taken off the host ([`flowback2.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/flowback2.json) `refugee_donor_funding`): UN refugee plans and EU grants paid for part of the cost in Moldova and others. Moldova's `round4b.json` figures are already its own cost, net of donors. Check 11 verifies this for Moldova.

4. **Net cost** (round 5, `apply_refugees_net()`, [`refugees_net.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees_net.json), [notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees_net-notes.md)): the cost minus the taxes and social contributions working refugees pay. Official revenue figures for Czechia (Labour Ministry: CZK 11 / 19 / 25bn in 2022–2024), Poland (Ministry of Finance, only the increase over a no-war path for Ukrainians already working there), Lithuania and Moldova; Germany from official job counts × wages × tax rate; elsewhere modelled (working-age share × employment rate × half the average wage × tax wedge, plus VAT). Across all hosts revenue offsets about half the cost over 2022–2026 (about 20% in 2022, over 60% in 2025). **Floored at zero on each country's 2022–2026 total**: where refugees paid more than they cost (Czechia, Canada, Slovakia, Romania, the Baltic states), hosting counts as zero, not as a shift toward Russia. Used in both the shift and the cost view.
5. **Poland** (round 5): the cost is refugee spending from the Aid Fund at BGK (PLN 11.3bn in 2022, 8.9bn in 2023, 8.1bn in 2024; NIK confirms 2022), not the Fund's total, which also pays for tasks outside Poland. Poland's 2022–2026 gross hosting cost fell from the Fund's total of EUR 24bn to EUR 10.8bn of refugee spending, and to about EUR 3.2bn net of refugees' taxes. At the time this moved Poland from +10.0% to +7.1% of GDP. Round 15 put Poland on the all-government definition (item 6). Bulgaria and Croatia also have new budget figures. Round 11 audit: these PLN figures were checked only against a press article, because the BGK and NIK pages blocked automated access ([verify_r11_aid-notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/verify_r11_aid-notes.md)), so Poland's refugee part is certainty C.

6. **One definition for every host** (round 15, review 4 M5, approved by the owner): the cost to **all levels of government** minus the taxes and contributions refugees pay. Germany's reported figure is federal spending and Poland's is the Aid Fund's refugee spending, so each is scaled by the gap that broad all-government estimates show for that host in 2022: Germany against the OECD *International Migration Outlook 2022* first-year estimate (EUR 6.8bn, 1.24 × federal) and Kiel's estimate (1.53 ×); Poland against OECD (EUR 8.36bn, 3.47 × the Fund's PLN 11.3bn), Kiel (3.68 ×) and Poland's Council for Cooperation with Ukraine (3.81 ×). The central value is the reported figure × √(median ratio), the rule round 5 used for Germany and Switzerland; the low end is the reported figure alone, the high end × the largest ratio. The OECD figures are as reported in the press ([Notes from Poland, 14 October 2022](https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/10/14/poland-to-spend-e8-4bn-supporting-ukraine-refugees-in-2022-highest-in-oecd/)); the OECD table itself blocked automated access (not verified). Effect at the time: Poland's hosting at net cost EUR 3.2bn → 9.6bn (support for Ukraine +1.11% → +1.95%), Germany's 29.5 → 25.1bn (+1.81% → +1.70%). **Round 16 (review 5 H1, approved by the owner): the gap applies to 2022 only**. It was measured on 2022 alone, and the OECD figure is a projection for 2022 with a fixed refugee population; Poland ended most free accommodation and host payments in 2023. From 2023 Poland keeps the Aid Fund's reported refugee spending with its own range; Germany's central values had round 5's gap built in for every year, so from 2023 its central is the reported federal figure and its high end keeps the gap. Poland's hosting at net cost EUR 9.6bn → 4.9bn, Germany's 25.1bn → 19.7bn; support for Ukraine now Poland +1.89%<!-- n:POL.ua -->, Germany +1.79%<!-- n:DEU.ua -->. Switzerland keeps its round-5 judgement (no broad estimate found); the modelled hosts keep the round-5 calibration. Refugees at gross cost are in the method band. The unverified Poland and Czechia figures that `kiel.json` carried from a search snippet were removed (they were never used). <span class="incode">In the code: `REFUGEE_SCOPE` in `build_data.py`; `REFUGEE_SCOPE_YEARS`; `SCOPE_IN_CENTRAL`</span>
7. **Not on the aggressor side** (round 15, raised by the owner). Russia is not on the map as a host: it is the aggressor, and the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants on 17 March 2023 against Vladimir Putin and Maria Lvova-Belova for the war crime of unlawful deportation and transfer of children from occupied areas of Ukraine to Russia ([ICC press release](https://www.icc-cpi.int/news/situation-ukraine-icc-judges-issue-arrest-warrants-against-vladimir-vladimirovich-putin-and)); the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on Ukraine found in March 2026 that these deportations and forcible transfers are crimes against humanity ([UN News, 12 March 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/03/1167122)). UNHCR's counts for Russia rest on Russian data. No refugee cost of Russia's is in any file the map uses, and `check_data.py` checks that Russia has no entry. **Belarus** hosted Ukrainian refugees too (EUR 0.11bn at net cost, certainty D), but it is a co-belligerent: the UN General Assembly "deplores the involvement of Belarus in this unlawful use of force against Ukraine" (resolution [ES-11/1](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/RES/ES-11/1&l=en&t=pdf), 2 March 2022). Its cost is moved out of support for Ukraine and every total into a label ("hosted Ukrainian refugees (€0.1bn), not counted as support: co-belligerent"; `ref_excluded` in `data.json`). The owner also noted that many Ukrainians reached Belarus via Russia; UNHCR's inter-agency update of October 2022 says most refugees in Belarus transited Poland, Lithuania and Latvia and "an unknown number continue to arrive via Russia" ([ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/belarus/regional-refugee-response-plan-ukraine-situation-inter-agency-operational-update-belarus-31-october-2022-enru)), so the decision rests on Belarus's role in the war, not on the route.

8. **Actual cost on the map, a common cost in the method band** (round 16, review 5 H2, approved by the owner). Each host keeps what it actually spent net of refugees' taxes: the owner chose actual cost. Because the same refugee is then worth EUR 0 to 20,000 a year depending on the host's welfare system, the method band adds "refugees at one common net cost per person-year for every host": each year's total net cost of all hosts over their person-years ([`refugees.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/refugees.json) `person_years`, 83 hosts; Belarus left out), EUR 5,780 per person-year in 2022, 3,693 in 2023, 2,180 in 2024, 1,322 in 2025 and 806 in January–August 2026 (`data.json` `meta`). Donor-paid shares stay off. The alternative counts in the "direction uncertain" test like every other one. <span class="incode">In the code: `refugee_common_cost()`, `calc(c, y, {refCommon: true})`</span>

The panel also shows the latest refugee count. <span class="incode">In the code: `people.refugees`</span>

### Refugee spending flowing back (round 16)

Raised by review 5 (M3); the owner asked for it. Like weapons bought at home, money spent hosting refugees is spent in the host's own economy: rent, food, retail and other services. Only the host's real gain on it is subtracted, under the same rule as other money flowing back ([one rule](#view=how&sec=m-overview-one-rule-for-every-seller-round-13)): the host's net gain per euro (taxes on production plus profit above a normal return) in the industries refugees' money flows through × the genuinely extra share of civilian spending ([`value_kept.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/value_kept.json) `flowback_other`). **Round 16b (check 16, N4; approved by the owner):** those industries are retail trade (STAN G47; all of G where G47 is missing), accommodation and food (I) and renting of real estate without owner-occupiers' imputed rent (L less L68A, where STAN publishes L68A); the mean of those the host reports ([`net_gain_sellers.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/net_gain_sellers.json) group `refugee_spending`; the OECD median outside STAN). Round 16 used the broad services group (I and GTNXL, business services without real estate), which holds finance (Poland's net gain 33%) and ICT, where refugees spend little. Poland is still the highest: its retail (29%) and accommodation and food (19%) keep a large operating surplus per unit of output, part of it the labour income of the self-employed that the correction for mixed income (self-employed × compensation per employee) does not fully take out; its rent without imputed rent is near zero (capital six times output). It is applied to the hosting cost counted (net of refugees' taxes) and shown as "Refugee hosting spent in its own economy (rent, food, services)" among the money flowing back; it is not part of the money that reached Ukraine. Weighted over all hosts it is 2.7% of the hosting cost (round 16b: Germany 2.2%, the Netherlands 3.0%, Poland 7.3%, was 8.2%); check 35 keeps it between 1% and 5%. <span class="incode">In the code: `refugee_flowback()`, `aid.refugee_back_f`</span>

## Military support to Russia

**Round 24b (audit 24, D M3; approved by the owner): North Korea revalued.** Its full value 2022 to August 2026 is EUR 10.0bn (6.8-18.6): low the Institute for National Security Strategy's (INSS, 16 March 2026) low end, central the INSS midpoint plus 2026, high the Korea Institute for Defense Analyses' (KIDA, April 2025) figure without the technology Russia gave North Korea, which is Russia's payment, not North Korea's delivery. Both studies were read only through press reports (NK News, Korea Times, Kyiv Independent). The paid share is unchanged (deliveries paid in kind count at the seller's gain); a method-band alternative counts only what was observed flowing back to Pyongyang (USD 0.5-1.5bn of fuel, food and weapons), which moves North Korea's net from about −11% to −32% of GDP. **Trade with Russia's two lines (round 24b):** the paid share of weapons sent to Russia is the seller's earning ("earned more from Russia"), the unpaid share a gift ("paid Russia more"). Data: [`north_korea_r24.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/north_korea_r24.json), [notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/north_korea_r24-notes.md).

"Weapons and troops sent to Russia", always toward Russia, certainty D:

- **North Korea**: shells, missiles and troops, from INSS munitions estimates, KIDA and others (central about EUR 9bn for August 2023 to December 2025; [other-notes.md](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/other-notes.md)); narrowed in [`narrow.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/narrow.json).
- **Iran**: Shahed and missile contracts, about EUR 4bn ([`flowback2.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/flowback2.json) `military_to_russia`).

## Flowback

Part of military aid comes back to the donor as orders for its own industry. Counting the full aid as a shift toward Ukraine while ignoring this would flatter big arms producers. Code: flowback section of `build()`; data: [`flowback.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/flowback.json) ([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/flowback-notes.md)) and [`flowback2.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/flowback2.json) ([notes](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/flowback2-notes.md)).

The first three bullets describe the estimates used up to round 5. **Since round 6 the all-money ledger below replaces all of them** (`apply_money_ledger()` deletes their values), so `OWN_SHARE_DEFAULT`, the PURL estimate and the EU arms slice no longer change any number on the map; they are kept as history.

- **Own aid spent with its own defence industry** (history): the own-industry share of bilateral military aid. US 0.86–0.90 (CSIS, certainty B); other donors from Kiel's item-level coding (domestic procurement + own stocks refilled at home + a share of uncoded value; Germany 0.40–0.59, UK 0.44–0.74, France 0.70–0.90, Netherlands 0.11–0.27); no evidence: 0% / 10% / 30% (`OWN_SHARE_DEFAULT`, round 11; it now applies only to Albania, North Macedonia and Montenegro, certainty D).
- **Other countries' aid spent here** (history; the EU arms split still feeds the ledger): NATO PURL purchases from US stocks (US receives EUR 3.7bn in 2025 and more in 2026), and EU-level military aid credited where Kiel's items say it was spent (round 11, `EU_ARMS_SLICE`): Sweden 28% (the Gripen contract), all other members together 3% (0–10%, split by the ASAP 2024 grants), and about 70% to Ukraine's own drone industry, which is no member's flowback. All of it is dated 2026, as in Kiel. Before round 11 the split followed SIPRI production shares (France 39%, Germany 24%, Italy 19%...).
- **Extra sales to Ukraine** above the expected level ([trade.md](#view=how&sec=m-trade)).

Until round 5 all three counted only as **profit** (20%, range 0.5–1.5×), the same share as profit lost on sales to Russia. Since round 6 they come from the all-money ledger below; from round 6 to round 12 they counted at value kept × genuinely extra ([the money rule](#view=how&sec=m-overview-the-money-rule-round-6)). **Since round 13 they count at the seller's net gain × genuinely extra** ([one rule](#view=how&sec=m-overview-one-rule-for-every-seller-round-13)): weapons at the defence industries' gain (round 13: US 0.032, Germany 0.037, OECD median 0.037; round 14, with a normal return of 5% a year above inflation plus the country's risk premium: US 0.051, Germany 0.051, OECD median 0.051), so US flowback falls from EUR 44bn to 1.5bn in round 13 and is EUR 2.5bn (3.6% of the EUR 70bn flowing back) in round 14 (the audit's point that replacing donated stock is defence spending, not income from Ukraine, is met by the rule: the US arms industry earns little above a normal return). Extra sales to Ukraine count at refining's and manufacturing's gain (Poland 0.68 × 0.116 + 0.32 × 0.066). They are subtracted from both the net shift and the cost. The "money flowing back" switch on the map (on by default, `state.netBack`) turns them off. Check 6 compares PURL paid with PURL received by the US.

### Round 6: the all-money ledger

Flowback now comes from one ledger of all money that reached Ukraine ([`money_ledger.json`](https://github.com/intelligent-cream/follow-the-money-ukraine/blob/main/research/2026-10/gaps/money_ledger.json),

balance with Ukraine was +50bn. Since round 13 the seller's net gain × genuinely extra applies: the US earns back
